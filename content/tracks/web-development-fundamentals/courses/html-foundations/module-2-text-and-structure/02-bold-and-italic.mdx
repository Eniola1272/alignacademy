# Bold & Italic

<Hook>
When you're texting a friend and want to emphasize something, you might write it in ALL CAPS or add exclamation marks!!! HTML gives you a more elegant way to do this — elements that tell the browser (and screen readers) "hey, this part is important" or "this part has emphasis."
</Hook>

## The Concept

Meet your two new best friends: **`<strong>`** and **`<em>`**.

`<strong>` marks text as **strongly important**. The browser renders it in **bold**. But it does more than just change the look — it tells screen readers and search engines that this text *matters*.

`<em>` adds **emphasis** to text. The browser renders it in *italics*. Screen readers will actually **change their tone of voice** when they encounter `<em>` — that's how your emphasis reaches users who can't see the screen.

You might be wondering: "What about `<b>` and `<i>`?" Good question. Those tags exist too, but they're **purely visual** — they make text bold or italic without adding any *meaning*. They're like putting on a costume without playing the part.

**Best practice: always use `<strong>` and `<em>`.** They convey meaning, not just appearance. This matters for accessibility — real people rely on screen readers every day.

You can even **nest them together** for text that's both bold *and* italic:

`<strong><em>really important</em></strong>`

Think of `<strong>` as raising your voice and `<em>` as leaning into a word. Sometimes you need both.

## The Syntax

```html
<p>
  Please <strong>do not</strong> touch the wet paint.
  It takes <em>at least</em> two hours to dry completely.
</p>

<p>
  This product is <strong><em>not suitable</em></strong> for children under 3.
</p>
```

In the first paragraph, "do not" is marked as strongly important — a clear warning. The word "at least" gets emphasis — it shifts the tone of the sentence. In the second paragraph, nesting both tags creates text that is bold AND italic.

## Try It Yourself

<CodeExercise language="html">
<Instructions>
In the recipe paragraph below, make "400 degrees" bold using `<strong>` and make "exactly" italic using `<em>`.
</Instructions>

<StartingCode>
<p>Preheat your oven to 400 degrees and wait for exactly 15 minutes.</p>
</StartingCode>

<Solution>
<p>Preheat your oven to <strong>400 degrees</strong> and wait for <em>exactly</em> 15 minutes.</p>
</Solution>

<Validation>
"400 degrees" should appear bold and "exactly" should appear in italics within the paragraph.
</Validation>
</CodeExercise>

## Recap

- ✅ `<strong>` makes text bold AND tells browsers it's important
- ✅ `<em>` makes text italic AND adds emphasis for screen readers
- ✅ Always prefer `<strong>`/`<em>` over `<b>`/`<i>` — meaning matters more than appearance
