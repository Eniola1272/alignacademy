# The Alt Attribute

<Hook>
Close your eyes and try to browse a website. That's the reality for millions of people who use screen readers every day. The `alt` attribute is your way of making images accessible to everyone — and it's also how Google "sees" your images. Two audiences, one small attribute.
</Hook>

## The Concept

You've already learned that the `alt` attribute provides a text description for an image. But writing *good* alt text is a skill. Let's go deeper.

**The golden rule:** Describe what the image **shows**, not what it **is**.

- 🚫 **Bad:** `alt="image"` or `alt="photo"`
- 🚫 **Bad:** `alt="dog"`
- ✅ **Good:** `alt="A golden retriever playing fetch in a sunny park"`

Be **specific and concise**. Paint a quick picture with words. Someone who can't see the image should understand what they're missing.

**Never start with "image of" or "picture of."** Screen readers already announce the element as an image before reading the alt text. Writing `alt="Image of a sunset"` would make the screen reader say *"Image: Image of a sunset"* — redundant and awkward.

**Decorative images** are a special case. If an image is purely visual decoration — a divider line, a background shape, a design flourish — it adds no meaningful information. For these, use an **empty alt attribute**: `alt=""`. This tells screen readers to skip the image entirely, so they don't clutter the experience with descriptions of meaningless graphics.

Alt text also shows up when **images fail to load** — maybe the URL is broken or the user has a slow connection. Instead of a blank space, they see your description. It's a helpful fallback.

And here's a bonus: **Google Image Search uses alt text** to understand and index your images. Good alt text means your images can appear in search results. That's better **SEO** (Search Engine Optimization) for free.

## The Syntax

```html
<!-- ✅ Good alt text — specific and descriptive -->
<img src="photo.jpg" alt="A golden retriever playing fetch in a sunny park">

<!-- 🚫 Bad alt text — vague and unhelpful -->
<img src="photo.jpg" alt="image">

<!-- ✅ Decorative image — empty alt to skip it -->
<img src="divider-line.png" alt="">
```

Notice how all three use the same `<img>` tag structure — the only difference is what's inside the `alt` attribute. That small change completely changes how screen readers, search engines, and broken-image fallbacks handle the image.

<Instructions>

## Recap

- ✅ Good alt text describes what the image shows — be specific and concise
- ✅ Use empty alt (`alt=""`) for decorative images that add no information
- ✅ Alt text helps screen readers, broken image fallbacks, and Google SEO — three wins in one
